# Pricing and Packaging Experiment

Date: 2026-05-20
Vertical: DFW emergency garage door repair
Status: buyer-discovery draft

## Pricing Principle

Do not price as "AI usage." Price against recovered booked jobs, faster lead response, and dispatcher/owner workload avoided.

The buyer should understand the offer as missed-revenue recovery with human-review safety, not a generic chatbot subscription.

## Price Hypotheses

| Package | Price hypothesis | Best buyer fit | Why it aligns with value | Risk to test |
|---------|------------------|----------------|--------------------------|--------------|
| Recovery Pilot | $500 setup plus $300/week for 2 weeks | Owner with clear missed-call/form problem but uncertain ROI | Small enough to approve manually; tied to proving recovered leads quickly | Buyer may see setup fee as friction before proof |
| Booked-Lead Share | $150 per incremental booked job, capped at $1,500/month during pilot | Buyer trusts booked-job tracking and has enough lead volume | Directly tracks recovered revenue; avoids paying for unconverted AI activity | Attribution disputes if buyer does not label outcomes cleanly |
| Dispatcher Assist | $750/month plus $2 per AI-handled lead after 300 leads | Buyer wants predictable monthly cost and lower review workload | Ties base fee to response coverage and variable fee to handled lead volume | Can feel like software cost if booked-job lift is not visible |

## Review Workload Adjustment

Pricing must account for human-review burden:

- If human-review rate stays under 30 percent and booked lift is visible, keep the package price.
- If human-review rate is 30-50 percent, discount or extend pilot while improving knowledge/policy coverage.
- If human-review rate is above 50 percent after week one, do not expand; the product is not yet reducing workload.

## Willingness-To-Pay Discovery Script

Ask about past behavior and tradeoffs:

1. In the last month, how many booked jobs do you believe were lost because response was too slow?
2. What is a typical gross revenue range for a spring, opener, cable, or stuck-door job?
3. What do you currently pay for call handling, missed-call recovery, ads, or lead services?
4. If the pilot recovered 3 booked jobs in 14 days with zero unsafe replies, what would that be worth monthly?
5. Would you prefer fixed monthly pricing, pay-per-booked-job, or a small base plus performance component?
6. What price would make this a no-brainer, what price would require approval, and what price would make you walk away?

## Success And Failure Criteria

Success:

- At least one buyer accepts written pilot terms, or
- At least one buyer gives a specific objection that can be addressed in the next offer version.

Failure:

- Buyers only respond to "AI curiosity" and cannot connect the offer to missed leads or bookings.
- Buyers refuse to share outcome labels, making performance pricing impossible.
- Review workload makes the offer look like extra admin rather than saved labor.
